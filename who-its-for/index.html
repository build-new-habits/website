Alongside: Move

Who it's for.

Your body. Your wellbeing. A coach that adapts to how you actually feel — not how you're supposed to feel. Built for people mainstream fitness culture has consistently failed: neurodivergent adults, people navigating burnout, hormonal change, or chronic conditions. Here are four of them — real reasons other apps didn't work, and what actually changes here.

Persona walkthroughs

Neurodivergent adult, ADHD, mid-30s

"I don't need another app that decides for me. I need one that actually notices what's going on."

What's been hard
  • Streaks, guilt notifications, and broken-chain messaging punish exactly the inconsistency her condition produces.
  • A small pool of workouts reshuffled to look varied reads as repetitive and triggers disengagement.
  • Long onboarding forms feel like rejection, not a task — each extra step multiplies exit risk.
How Alongside helps
  • FreeCredits, not streaks — nothing resets, nothing is broken by a missed week.
  • FreeA short, conversational check-in — energy, mood, pain, time — not a form.
  • PersonalThe other six session types open up — Glute Focus, Upper Body, Cardio, and more — so a session can genuinely look different, not the same Full Body slot every time.
  • PersonalThe coach starts noticing her own patterns — when a hyperfocus curve usually breaks — instead of only responding to today.
How it felt, over time

Three weeks in, she noticed the coach hadn't asked her to explain the gap the one week she'd disappeared. It just picked up where she'd left off. That was the moment it stopped feeling like every other app she'd abandoned.

Woman navigating perimenopause, late 40s

"I'm not looking to push through this. I'm looking for something that finally takes it seriously."

What's been hard
  • Mainstream fitness apps offer no menopause support, or tracking only — nothing that actually changes what's recommended.
  • Rigid 28-day cycle logic doesn't fit an irregular or absent cycle.
  • Wellness marketing implicitly says push harder, rather than validating that today is genuinely different from yesterday.
How Alongside helps
  • FreeCoaching tone and exercise selection adapt to perimenopause/menopause status and symptoms.
  • FreeNo assumed cycle pattern — the daily check-in drives adaptation, not a fixed 28-day model.
  • FreeCycle tracking is optional, never required — skip it with no penalty.
  • PersonalTrend view shows how her energy has actually moved week to week, not just today.
  • PersonalThe coach starts naming patterns across weeks, not just reacting to each day in isolation.
How it felt, over time

She'd stopped expecting anyone, let alone an app, to take the unpredictability seriously. What changed her mind wasn't a big feature — it was the coach adjusting a session on a bad brain-fog day without her having to explain why.

Woman in general fitness, early 30s

"I don't want a transformation. I want something that doesn't make me feel like I've failed every time life gets in the way."

What's been hard
  • Apps built on streaks and comparison pushed her into over-exertion followed by guilt-driven abandonment.
  • Most fitness content quietly reduces movement to appearance management.
  • Even as a younger user, cycle-linked energy shifts are ignored by mainstream apps.
How Alongside helps
  • FreePattern-detection triggers Recovery Mode before burnout lands.
  • FreeCycle-phase adaptation applies to regular cycles too, not just perimenopause.
  • FreeNo before/after, no calorie language, anywhere in the product.
  • PersonalEvery session type and every duration, so a workout can actually match how much time and energy she has that day — not always the same 30-minute slot.
  • PersonalProgress is framed as consistency and trend, never a number to chase.
  • PersonalA generative programme builds week on week toward her own goals, instead of always starting fresh.
How it felt, over time

The thing that stayed with her wasn't a single workout — it was going back after a rough two weeks and finding nothing had reset. No lost streak to mourn. Just the next session, waiting.

  • FreeExercise history and "returning after" context let onboarding build from his real background.
  • FreeCondition filtering and burnout detection are free forever — never a feature he has to pay to access safely.

He'd expected another blanket disclaimer. What he got instead was a plan that actually knew which exercises to avoid for his back — and, a few months on, one that had quietly moved him past where he'd stalled the last two times he tried.

Free vs Personal.

Safety and wellbeing are never part of this comparison — they're standard at every tier, always.

Sessions
A session is one workout — the exercises the coach puts together for you to do in one sitting. There are seven kinds, each focused on something different (like Full Body, Upper Body, or Cardio). On Free, you always get the Full Body kind, at a fixed 30 minutes. On Personal, you choose which of the seven you want, and how long you've got.
Exercise library
This is the full set of exercises the coach can choose from — hundreds of movements, each rated from easier to harder. On Personal, the coach matches exercises to your level and moves you up as you're ready, instead of leaving that to guesswork.
Programmes
A programme is how your sessions connect to each other over time, not just what happens in one sitting. On Personal, the coach builds a programme that progresses week by week — not just a fresh session each time, but one that's going somewhere.
Progress
How much insight you get into your own patterns. On Free, you see the last 7 days. On Personal, you can look back 30 or 90 days too, and the coach points out patterns it's noticed.
Coach behaviour
On Free, the coach responds to how you are today. On Personal, it also remembers — noticing patterns across weeks and bringing them up, not just reacting in the moment.
